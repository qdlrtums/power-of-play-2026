A block

Four stages, always in this order

The goal never changes between them. The game changes constantly.

  1. Play assessment

    Visit 1 · 60 minutes

    The child plays and the therapist watches. We map what they can do, what they avoid, and what they love, before setting a single goal.

  2. Goals, written as play

    Visit 2 · with caregivers

    Every clinical goal is restated as something the child would want to do anyway. "Tolerates prone position" becomes "defends the fort on their elbows".

  3. The block

    Visits 3–15 · weekly

    Sessions built around whatever the child is currently obsessed with. Dinosaurs one month, diggers the next; the goal holds steady.

  4. Handover

    Visit 16 · and after

    The adults who see the child daily leave knowing which games do the work, so progress doesn't stop when the block does.

Who it's for

Built for the people already in the room

Families

Therapy your child asks to come back to, and a home programme built out of games you're already playing on the kitchen floor.

Referring pediatricians

Structured goals, measured progress, and a discharge summary that states plainly what changed, what didn't, and what to watch.

Preschools and schools

Sessions that can run in the classroom instead of pulling a child out of it, and staff who leave able to keep the work going.

Researchers

De-identified block data in a documented schema, with the goal-scoring rubric versioned alongside it.
