The session

Four stages, in order. Nothing about the room changes between them — only what the clinician is watching for.

  1. Free play

    Minutes 0–8 · no prompts

    The child chooses the materials. The clinician records what gets picked up, what gets ignored, and how long attention holds without adult scaffolding.

  2. Invitation

    Minutes 8–14 · one prompt per domain

    A single open-ended offer — a nesting set, a doll and a cup — probes the domains free play didn't surface on its own.

  3. Caregiver turn

    Minutes 14–20 · clinician steps back

    The caregiver plays; the clinician observes the pair. Serve-and-return patterns are scored here, never in the clinician-led stages.

  4. Record and route

    Same day

    Observations resolve into a scored profile with an explicit next step: continue, re-screen at a named age, or refer with the evidence attached.

For pediatric clinics

Screening that fits a well-child visit, produces a chartable record, and gives you defensible grounds for a referral — or for holding off on one.

For early intervention teams

A shared vocabulary across OT, SLP, and behavioural staff, so a handoff carries the observation itself and not just the score.

For families

A plain-language summary of what the session showed, what it didn't, and what to look for at home before the next visit.

For researchers

De-identified session data, exportable in a documented schema, with the scoring rubric versioned alongside it.
