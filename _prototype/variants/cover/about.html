Why play

Conventional pediatric rehab asks a small child to repeat a movement they find hard, on demand, in a room they didn't choose to be in. It works, when they comply. The children who don't comply — and at three years old, most don't — get recorded as poor attenders rather than as children whose therapy was badly designed for them.

Play is not a sweetener we add to the therapy. It is the mechanism. A child defending a fort on their elbows holds a prone position far longer than one asked to hold a prone position. Our work is designing the game so the repetitions happen, counting them honestly, and being straight about what changed.

What this is not

Power of Play is a rehabilitation service, not a diagnostic one. We work from a referral question someone else has established, and we say so plainly when a child needs assessment we can't provide.
