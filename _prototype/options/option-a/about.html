About

We started in a waiting room with a bin of blocks.

Power of Play began as a scoring sheet two clinicians kept photocopying because the intake form had nowhere to record what they were actually seeing.

Standardised screening instruments ask a caregiver to remember whether a child does something. They are quick, they are validated, and they miss the child in front of you. Meanwhile the most informative twenty minutes of the visit — the child playing while the adults talk — goes unrecorded because there is no structure for recording it.

Our work is that structure. Not a new theory of development, and not a replacement for the instruments your practice already trusts: a way to make direct observation count as data, with the same rigour those instruments get held to.

The method

Rubric v4.2, published February 2026.

What gets scored, and against what

Six domains, each anchored to observable behaviour rather than caregiver report. Every score in the record links back to the specific moment in the session that produced it.

Scored domains and their observation anchors
Domain Code Observed in Norm source
Symbolic play SYM Free play Lifter, 2023
Joint attention JA Invitation Mundy, 2016
Fine motor sequencing FMS Free play, invitation Bayley-4
Expressive language EXP All stages MB-CDI
Serve and return S&R Caregiver turn Internal, v4
Frustration recovery FR Invitation Internal, v4
What this is not

Power of Play does not diagnose, and no score it produces is a diagnostic threshold. A profile that flags a domain is grounds for a re-screen or a referral — never for a label.

Evidence

Where the numbers come from

1,208 Sessions in the validation set 41 clinics, 9 states
0.84 Inter-rater agreement across scored domains Krippendorff's α
11% Of sessions changed the referral decision Versus checklist alone

The validation set skews urban and English-dominant. We say so plainly because it bounds what the norms can currently support, and a second cohort recruiting through community health centres is meant to address it. Cohort two opens September 2026.
